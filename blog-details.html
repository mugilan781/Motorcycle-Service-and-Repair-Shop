Track Day Prep Checklist: 12 Points Before You Ride

Aug 02, 2026 6 min read Race Prep Crew

Every spring, the same scene repeats at scrutineering: a rider with a fast bike, fresh leathers — and coolant weeping from an over-tightened drain bolt. Track days don't fail on horsepower. They fail on details. This is the literal sheet our Race package techs sign before a machine leaves for circuit duty.

Why a Written Checklist Beats Confidence

Memory is a terrible torque wrench. Under time pressure the brain happily skips step nine and swears it didn't. A printed sheet, initialed per line, turns twelve good intentions into twelve verifiable actions — which is exactly what a paddock marshal wants to see when something drips.

The fastest people at any track day are not the riders. They are the ones who finished prep on Thursday night.

The Twelve Points

Ours breaks into three groups of four: containment (keeping fluids in), control (keeping the bike pointed correctly) and confirmation (proving both under load).

  • Coolant replaced with water/wetter, system pressure-tested warm
  • Drain plugs, filter and filler cap torqued and safety-wired where required
  • Brake pads above 50% with fresh fluid, full bleed, lever feel logged
  • Tyres at cold pressures written on the rim tape for the day's ambient temp
  • Chain tensioned to spec, alignment verified by laser, rivet link inspected
  • Suspension sag set for rider weight in gear; clickers photographed as baseline
  • Mirrors removed or taped, reflectors and number plate stowed legally
  • Throttle free play within spec, snaps back cleanly bar-to-bar
  • Battery secured, terminals dielectric-greased, charging voltage verified
  • ECU fault log read and cleared; no pending codes carried onto pit lane
  • Axles, pinch bolts and rear stand pick-ups re-torqued after warm-up lap one
  • Post-session hot tyre pressures recorded — data you'll want all season

The Part Everyone Skips: Post-Day Care

A track day costs your bike more than the laps suggest. Brake fluid absorbs moisture faster at race temps, chains stretch measurably in one hard afternoon, and tyres that look fine can have cord showing at the edge of the contact patch. Book a check-over within the week — our post-track inspection is $49 and takes ninety minutes.

Key Takeaways

  • Prep fails on fluids and fasteners — wire anything that spins.
  • Write settings down before you change them; baselines save seasons.
  • The week after matters as much as the Friday before.
